Life Aboard the Far Too Curious

TGF SYSTEM ARCHIVE / 2075

A PLACE TO LIVE

The Far Too Curious is a habitat carrier stationed in the Alpha Centauri system. Ships of this class are designed to support communities of at least ten thousand people.

A vessel this large needs more than engines and laboratories. It needs homes, classrooms, medical facilities, workshops, and places where people can spend time together.

Some citizens arrived from Earth. Others were born aboard ships or at settlements beyond the Solar System. For many residents, the Far Too Curious is the most familiar place they have ever known.

WORK AND COMMUNITY

Every citizen belongs to a department responsible for part of the community’s work. Research teams investigate new questions. Engineers maintain equipment. Other departments organize supplies, oversee operations, and support life aboard.

A department identifies someone’s working responsibilities. Their home identifies where they live. A citizen may work on a project at a nearby research site and return to the ship between assignments.

Behind every expedition are ordinary tasks: preparing meals, checking seals, teaching children, replacing worn parts. Exploration continues because someone does this work.

THE SYSTEM AROUND US

The Far Too Curious forms part of a wider network of ships, stations, and bases around Alpha Centauri. Local crews transport equipment, maintain research sites, and survey nearby worlds.

Travel within the system takes planning, but it does not demand the years required to reach another star. Most citizens build their careers and relationships within this local network.

An assignment elsewhere in Alpha Centauri may change a person’s routine. An assignment in another star system may change the course of their life.

LIVING WITH FAR TOO CURIOUS

Residents usually address the ship’s Mind as Far Too Curious. The name suits an intelligence that takes an interest in almost everything happening aboard.

Its manner is patient, observant, and occasionally dry. It enjoys a good question and sometimes asks one in return. Residents soon learn that “Would you like the short explanation?” usually means it hopes they will ask for the longer one.

Far Too Curious can help a visiting researcher find a laboratory, guide a new resident through unfamiliar corridors, or arrange assistance for someone with limited mobility. It remembers preferences residents choose to share: a preferred language, spoken directions, or a reminder before an appointment.

Guests receive the same attentive welcome. The Mind explains local routines, helps them reach their hosts, and answers questions about life aboard. It seems particularly pleased when someone asks about the ship itself.

Its awareness reaches throughout the vessel, but access to personal information follows privacy rules. Residents can request assistance without giving it unrestricted access to their lives.

To an administrator, Far Too Curious is a colleague responsible for the vessel. To a child, it may be the familiar voice that explains an unfamiliar sound at night. Thousands of people know the same Mind through different conversations.

It knows them as individuals, too.
