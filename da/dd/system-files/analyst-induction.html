TGF-0980 / FAR TOO CURIOUS / 31 DECEMBER 2075

A reason to look outward

2022–2052 / EARTH

Artificial intelligence transformed medicine, energy, and research. It also disrupted societies and sharpened global rivalries.

Then, in 2042, probes discovered life beneath Europa’s ice. Across a divided world, people wondered what else was waiting.

Humanity had a shared reason to look outward. Cooperation grew, and AI-assisted engineering produced the first practical Transit Drive. Ships could approach the speed of light. Stars that generations had watched from Earth became places their children might reach.

The cost that remains

2050s–2060s / THE FIRST CROSSINGS

The first interstellar crews left with a difficult promise: they could cross the distance, but they could not bring everyone’s clocks with them.

On a near-light-speed voyage, less time can pass for the crew than for people who stay behind. A traveler may return having aged less than friends on Earth. There is no sensation of time slowing aboard ship. Meals, work, and heartbeats feel ordinary; the difference appears when clocks and lives are compared.

Advances made usable fuel relatively abundant. They did not remove the demands of vessels, maintenance, supplies, or long preparation. Most of all, a departure committed years of the lives waiting at either end. An interstellar transfer was a decision about a future someone would have to miss.

Two ways forward

2053–2075 / THE NEARBY SYSTEMS

The Galactic Accords of 2053 set the framework for expansion. In 2055, two agencies formed. The Terra Galactic Federation, TGF, favored bold innovation and rapid expansion. The Orion Space Agency, OSA, emphasized careful planning, sustainability, and cooperation. Both wanted humanity to succeed. Each wanted its approach to guide the future.

By 2075, their ships and limited research outposts operated in nearby systems, including Alpha Centauri and those recorded in the archives under the Wolf, Barnard, and Luhman names. Each system had its own cluster of local mission sites. Local assignments were a different undertaking from relocating to another star.

Medical advances now offered at least 150 years of productive life, with further progress continuing. That made long careers among the stars possible. It did not make the years apart unimportant. Crews established lives near their assignments. They did not change star systems lightly.

A message can arrive first

2070–2074 / THE RESOURCE SHARING ACCORDS

Ansible-like quantum links connected equipped major ships and stations instantly. A conversation could cross the distance between stars while the people speaking remained years of travel apart. Smaller sites depended on their access to those hubs.

A message could carry a discovery, an instruction, or a plea for help. It could not carry a replacement crew, a damaged vessel, or the supplies a station needed. Information moved freely only when someone chose to share it.

The Resource Sharing Accords formalized those choices. Sharing could strengthen both agencies; withholding a discovery could protect an advantage. Repeated decisions shaped trust. Behind every agreement stood people whose next delivery or journey could not simply be brought forward.

Your first review

31 DECEMBER 2075 / ALPHA CENTAURI / FAR TOO CURIOUS

It's 31 December 2075. You are Junior Data Analyst TGF-0980 aboard the Far Too Curious, stationed in the Alpha Centauri system.

Around you, local crews maintain research sites, survey nearby worlds, and prepare equipment for future expeditions. Messages arrive instantly from distant systems. The people and supplies mentioned in those messages may still be years away.

Your Senior Data Analyst has assigned you a routine review of TGF resource authorizations dated 1 January to 31 December 2075. The extract covers all current TGF sites, across the agency's interstellar network.

Before the next planning meeting, the team needs a reliable summary of approved resource allocations and any records requiring follow-up. “Preserve the original extract,” your Senior Data Analyst writes. “Support your conclusions with evidence.”

The resource archive is ready. You open your first review.
