Two Agencies, One Frontier

TGF SYSTEM ARCHIVE / 2075

A SHARED BEGINNING

The discovery of life beneath Europa’s ice gave humanity a common reason to explore. Cooperation accelerated advances in spacecraft engineering and helped make the nearby stars reachable.

It did not settle who should direct that expansion, how quickly it should proceed, or how its benefits should be shared.

The Galactic Accords of 2053 established a framework for cooperation beyond the Solar System. Within that framework, disagreements continued. In 2055, two agencies formed.

TWO APPROACHES

The Terra Galactic Federation favored bold innovation and rapid expansion. Its supporters believed that developing new capabilities and establishing sites quickly would secure humanity’s future.

The Orion Space Agency emphasized careful planning and sustainable exploration. Its supporters believed that expansion would succeed only if communities could maintain what they built and understand the consequences of their actions.

Both approaches carried risks. Speed could exceed preparation. Caution could delay a discovery or assistance that people needed.

Neither agency spoke with one voice. Researchers, crews, and administrators could disagree with their own leaders as strongly as they disagreed with the other agency.

RULES FOR COOPERATION

Between 2070 and 2074, the Resource Sharing Accords established rules for exchanging discoveries, supplies, and access to exploration sites.

The agreements helped the agencies coordinate work without requiring them to surrender every independent interest. Shared activity existed alongside separate projects and restricted research.

An agreement could establish who was entitled to request assistance. Fulfilling that request still depended on available equipment, local capacity, and the time needed to deliver it.

DEPENDENCE AND DISTRUST

Across the nearby systems, crews sometimes relied on people working under the other agency’s authority. They exchanged supplies, shared research, and helped one another through emergencies.

Their leaders also competed for influence and guarded discoveries that might give their agency an advantage.

Cooperation could build trust through repeated, practical acts. A withheld result or broken commitment could undo it. People working side by side did not always share the suspicions of officials years away.

THE FRONTIER IN 2075

TGF and OSA now operate across several nearby star systems. Quantum communications allow immediate discussion, but ships and supplies still take years to cross between stars.

That distance gives local decisions lasting consequences. A discovery may benefit both agencies, strengthen one agency’s position, or create risks that neither can manage alone.

The accords provide a framework. People must still decide what to share, what to protect, and what evidence justifies their choice.
