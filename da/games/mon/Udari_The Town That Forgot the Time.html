Chapter I: The Forgotten Town

THE BEGINNING

You arrive in the quiet town of Bellwether just before midnight. The streets are empty, every clock shows a different time, and the old station has been abandoned for years.

Nobody in town remembers why time stopped being reliable. The town records are incomplete, the station clock is frozen, and the last train disappeared from local history.

An elderly station keeper finds you near the platform and places a sealed letter in your hand.

"Find the right bus. Find the right time. Only then will the town remember."

✉ The Hidden Letter

EVIDENCE #01

To the One Who Still Remembers

Dear Stranger,

If this letter has reached you, Bellwether has forgotten again.

Three buses leave the old station every night. One follows the river road, one crosses the mountain, and one passes the abandoned clock tower.

Do not trust the earliest bus. Do not trust the road that looks shortest.

The correct bus leaves when the clock tower strikes thirteen minutes past nine. Its number is hidden in the date the town lost its time.

The town lost its time on October 14, 1987. Add the digits of that date until only one digit remains. Then add the number of the month.

When you find the right bus and the correct departure, ask why it was the only one allowed to leave that night.

Do not leave before you understand what happened. The answer is more important than the journey.

— The Last Station Keeper
Detective's note: The letter contains clues about the town's forgotten date, a bus number, a departure time, and a secret reason. Read carefully.

The Night Time Disappeared

You have discovered the correct bus and departure time. The station keeper finally opens the second envelope.

In October 1987, Bellwether depended on its old clock tower to synchronize the station, the town hall, and the emergency warning system.

On the night of October 14, the clock tower mechanism failed during a severe storm. The town's official records were damaged, and different buildings began recording different times.

A midnight evacuation had been scheduled, but the broken clocks caused confusion. Families missed departures, messages arrived too late, and the town's history was recorded incorrectly.

The station keeper discovered that Route 32 was the only bus authorized to carry the original time records to the regional archive. Its departure was delayed until the clock tower's final signal could be verified.

The unusual departure at 9:13 PM was not random. It matched the recorded signal time in the station's surviving log.

The keeper hid the letter so that someone would reconstruct the clues instead of accepting the town's incomplete official story.

The truth: Bellwether did not literally forget how time works. Its people lost trust in their clocks and records after one night of confusion. The mystery was preserved by a hidden letter and a forgotten bus timetable.
